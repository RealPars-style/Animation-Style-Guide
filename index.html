Animation style guide

One calm look for every video. Black, white and grey, with one highlighted part at a time.

  • Frame1920 × 1080
  • Frame rate30 fps
  • FontInter
  • Background#0b0b0c

The idea

Our videos use one calm look. Black, white and grey only. No brand colors.

The drawing does not change color to get attention. Movement and one highlight do that job. At any moment, only one part of the drawing is bright. Everything else is dimmed. The viewer always knows where to look.

The same system repeats for every topic in the video. Only the highlighted part changes.

Background and theme

The animation is always dark. Use the near-black background #0b0b0c for every animated scene. Do not use a white or grey background, and do not mix dark and light scenes in the animation.

Why dark:

  • The highlight system needs it. On a dark background, the highlighted part really lights up and the eye goes straight to it. On white, the dimmed parts look washed out.
  • It looks calm and premium, like a product film, not a slide deck.
  • Most people watch YouTube in dark mode, and a dark frame is easier on the eyes for a 6 to 7 minute video.

Do not use pure black #000000. It looks harsh and shows compression artifacts on YouTube.

Real footage and the presenter on camera can stay natural. They do not need to be dark.

For the article images on the blog, use a white or very light grey background, for example #f5f5f7. The drawings are the same as in the video. Only the background changes.

Drawing style

The hardware is drawn as clean, flat technical drawings. They must be correct, not artistic.

ItemRuleSizePreview
Corner radius, large parts Simple shapes with soft rounded corners About 12 to 18 px
Corner radius, small parts Simple shapes with soft rounded corners About 4 to 6 px
Line thickness Thin lines 1 to 1.5 px

Previews are drawn at the real pixel sizes.

TopicRule
ShadeOnly a very soft top to bottom shade on large surfaces. No glows, no strong shadows, no 3D effects.
Text and logosNo fake text, logos or brand names on the hardware. Label plates stay blank.
ScreensScreens show only a few quiet grey lines, never a busy picture.

The soft shade

Large surfaces are not one flat grey. They are a little lighter at the top and a little darker at the bottom. This makes the part look like it has light coming from above, without any 3D effect.

How to make it:

StepWhat to do
1Fill the shape with a linear gradient.
2Set the direction straight down, from top to bottom (90 degrees). Never sideways or at an angle.
3Use only two color stops, one at the very top (0%) and one at the very bottom (100%). No stops in the middle.
4The top color is the lighter one. The bottom color is the darker one.
5Keep the step small. The two colors must be close, so the viewer feels it more than sees it.

Use it only on large surfaces, like the panel body, the housing and the front bezel. Do not use it on small parts (screws, ports, clamps), on lines, on text, or on the background. Those stay flat.

When a part is highlighted, it turns flat white #f5f5f7 with no shade. When it is dimmed, the shade stays and the whole part goes to about 40% opacity.

The highlight system

This is the heart of the style. When the presenter talks about one part, that part is highlighted like this:

StepWhat happens
1Everything else fades down to about 40% opacity.
2The part we talk about turns bright white (#f5f5f7). Its dark details stay dark (#1d1d1f), so it is still easy to read.
3A thin white outline (1 px) appears around the part, with a small gap.
4A thin white leader line (1.5 px) draws from the part to the text, with a small dot at the part end.
5The title and sub text fade in next to the line.

Only one part is highlighted at a time. To move to the next part, reverse the steps on the old part, then run them on the new one.

The highlight is never a color. It is always white on the dimmed grey drawing.

Motion and timing

Every highlight follows the same beat. These are the times from the sample video. Stretch the hold to match the voice over, but keep the other steps the same length.

TimeWhat happens
0.0 to 1.0 sThe drawing fades in from black
1.0 to 2.0 sThe whole drawing holds in normal grey
2.0 to 2.8 sThe rest dims, the part turns white, the outline fades in
2.8 to 3.6 sThe leader line draws from the part to the text
3.4 to 4.4 sThe text fades in and moves up 10 px
4.4 s and onHold while the presenter talks

Rules:

TopicRule
CurveAll moves use a smooth ease in and ease out curve. No linear moves, no bounce.
Push inThrough the whole shot, a very slow push in (about 3% zoom over the shot) keeps the frame alive.
One move at a timeMove only one thing at a time, except where the table shows an overlap.
StyleNo spinning, flying in or flashy transitions. Calm and slow beats fast.

ItemRule

DrawingSits on the right half.

Logos and marksNo logos, website address or watermarks inside the animation.
